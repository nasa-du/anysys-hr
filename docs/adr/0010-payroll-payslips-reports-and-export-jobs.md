# Payroll payslips, department summaries, and export jobs

Payroll needs to issue payslips and produce monthly reports from results that the lifecycle contract already treats as overwritten current state plus immutable paid snapshots. The requirement asks for a personal payslip grouped by the employee's calculation scheme with four totals, a department summary, and Excel/PDF exports, but the payroll model has one global field definition (visibility and order), calculation schemes are formula containers only, and the paid snapshot is the only value-level history. The PDF research measured WeasyPrint with static TrueType Noto Sans SC as the lowest-integration-cost server-side path and recommended per-employee PDFs plus a merged document, but left the data source, field grouping, report boundaries, audit, encryption, and batch ceiling open. The product chose a 1000-employee batch ceiling, which exceeds a comfortable synchronous HTTP budget and therefore requires a durable export job rather than an in-request render.

We chose a **read-only payslip projection with two modes**: an **official payslip** rendered from the latest paid snapshot whenever one exists (unlocking or recalculating a paid month does not change the issued version; paying again appends a new snapshot that becomes official), and a **preview payslip** rendered from the current successful calculation run for a month in calculated or confirmed, marked in the header as not issued. Official and preview are separate export modes and are never mixed in one batch. A preview job pins the current successful run at creation and fails with `source_changed` if the month's successful run or preview eligibility changes before rendering; an official job pins the snapshot id. A month with no successful run cannot produce a payslip. A paid month produces only official payslips; preview is available for calculated and confirmed months, and a month that needs recalculation shows that state on every preview page, together with the month status and a note when the most recent calculation run failed.

We chose to derive payslip presentation from the **global field definition**: fields are grouped by category (earning, addon, deduction, reference) and ordered by display_order, only visible fields appear, and custom fields appear automatically in their category. Unpaid months use the current definitions; paid months use the labels, order, and visibility frozen in the paid snapshot. This deliberately interprets the requirement's "grouped by calculation scheme" as "the scheme determines the employee's effective formulas, not the display", because a per-scheme display configuration would be a second source of visibility and order and would contradict the single field definition. Total fields are not repeated as ordinary rows: normal_pay, addon_sum, and deduct_sum are group subtotals, the summary block shows gross, social insurance, tax, and net, and advances remain in the deduction group. Payslips are A4 portrait, one page per employee by default, with a repeated table header and no truncation when content continues.

We chose **server-side WeasyPrint with Jinja2 and vendored static Noto Sans SC TrueType fonts**, retaining per-employee PDFs, a merged one-person-per-page PDF, and a flat payroll-detail Excel export; the batch payslip Excel is the same flat table, not a workbook per employee. Batch selection uses the payroll-list filter (department including descendants through the month's frozen department paths, position, keyword) or an explicit employee subset, with zero matches rejected. Department summaries aggregate by a **frozen department path** captured per result at calculation, not by the current organization tree; parent rows include descendants, the company row is the total, and the columns are headcount, gross, social insurance, tax, advances, and net. Paid months summarize the latest paid snapshot; other months summarize the current successful run and display the month status. Department summaries export as Excel only; the bank payment/transfer sheet is out of scope.

We chose a **unified export record with asynchronous batch rendering**. Single payslips and Excel exports are synchronous. Batch payslips create a `payroll_export` row (the same table records synchronous exports and acts as the export audit), are claimed by a dedicated worker service through PostgreSQL (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), render in a process pool one job at a time, and write artifacts atomically to a shared named volume mounted read-write in the worker and read-only in the backend. The hard ceiling is 1000 employees per batch, artifacts expire 24 hours after completion, metadata is permanent, expired downloads return 410, and worker housekeeping removes expired artifacts and orphans. Artifacts are temporary delivery buffers, never a PDF archive: official payslips are regenerated from the paid snapshot, and the export record stores the template version. The performance budgets are a single PDF under one second p95, a 1000-employee ZIP within 60 seconds p95, a merged 1000-page PDF within 120 seconds p95, and an end-to-end job within three minutes. Access is login-only for every payroll action, without PDF/ZIP passwords or PDF/A in this phase; responses are no-store, and the audit records the operator, scope, employee count, data source, template version, and outcome without amounts. Batch-job downloads update the row's download counter and last downloader rather than appending per-download records; each synchronous single-payslip download is its own export record.

Alternatives rejected: browser/frontend batch printing (unreproducible, unauditable, and requires the payroll data in the browser), Chromium/Playwright (deployment weight and Chinese text extraction defects), ReportLab (hand-written layout and a second rendering stack), synchronous batch rendering at 1000 (HTTP and proxy timeouts, no progress), database `bytea` artifacts (larger backups and a second large-object store), permanent PDF archives (a second value-level history), per-scheme display configuration (a second source of visibility and order), current-tree department summaries (silently rewrite history after a reorganisation), and a bank payment sheet (out of scope).

## Consequences

- Issued payslips are immutable documents; corrections after payment go through unlock, unconfirm, recalculate, confirm, pay again, and a new snapshot version.
- Results and the paid snapshot gain a frozen department path; department summaries stay stable across department moves, renames, and deletions.
- Preview exports can fail with `source_changed` when the month is recalculated between job creation and rendering; the user recreates the export rather than receiving a different set of numbers.
- The deployment gains a worker service and a shared named volume; the volume holds PII for at most 24 hours and is excluded from backups.
- 1000-employee batches no longer depend on an HTTP connection staying open; single PDF and Excel exports remain synchronous.
- The export audit is one table, one row per export request or job, with no amounts; download history is summarized for batch jobs.
- PDF/ZIP encryption, PDF/A, permanent PDF archives, bank payment sheets, employee self-service, and per-scheme display configuration remain out of scope.
